# Things and kinds

This chapter is about sorting. You learn to tell a kind of thing from one example of it, how to stack kinds into a tree, and how to say which kinds can overlap. Read it when you're about to decide what your classes are.

## What you should know

- A [knowledge graph](knowledge-graphs.md) stores facts as things joined by named links.
- An [ontology](../GLOSSARY.md#concept-ontology) is the plan that says which kinds of things exist and how they may connect.
- A [competency question](../GLOSSARY.md#concept-competency-question) is a question your ontology must answer, and the questions tell you which kinds you need.

## The pages

- [What are classes and instances?](classes-and-instances.md) answers whether a thing is a kind or an example.
- [What does "is a kind of" mean?](hierarchy.md) answers how kinds stack so that facts are written once.
- [Can a drink be two kinds at once?](disjoint-and-multiple-parents.md) answers when kinds can't overlap and when one may have two parents.
